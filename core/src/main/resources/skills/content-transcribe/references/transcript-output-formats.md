# How to write the various kinds of results

Always write times as "minutes:seconds", and as "hours:minutes:seconds" beyond one hour. Put "approx." before estimated times.

## Summary

Five to ten sentences, third person, present tense, making clear the topic, main arguments and conclusion of this piece of content. Add no evaluation.

## Chapter list

One time plus a short title per line, with the title no longer than fifteen characters:

```
00:00 Opening and topic
03:40 Why this problem exists
09:15 Comparison of the two approaches
21:30 Actual data
35:05 Listener questions
```

## Chapter-by-chapter summary

After the chapter title, write in parentheses roughly how long the chapter is, and below it two or three sentences on what the chapter actually covers:

```
### 03:40 Why this problem exists (about 5 minutes)
The speaker reviewed the origin of this problem and gave two common examples.

### 09:15 Comparison of the two approaches (about 12 minutes)
Compared the cost and effect of approach A and approach B, and the conclusion is...
```

## Quotes

Record the original words verbatim, followed by the time they appear; note whether it is the subtitle original or a translation:

```
"Clean the data first; that matters more than changing the model." —— 12:18
```

## Rewriting into an article

- One title
- An opening paragraph saying which episode of content this is compiled from
- A subheading for each main topic, with narrative paragraphs below it
- Key original words in quote format, with times
- A closing paragraph: the key points, or advice that can be followed
